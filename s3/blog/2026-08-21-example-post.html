August 21, 2026 Puzzle Breakdown

Four groups, four words each — here's how today's puzzle connects.

Fruits people mistake for vegetables

TOMATOES, CUCUMBERS, EGGPLANTS, AVOCADOS

All four are botanically fruits — they develop from a flower and carry seeds — but every one of them gets cooked, sliced, and shelved like a vegetable. It's the easiest group in today's puzzle because the words themselves aren't doing any misdirection; the trick is entirely in the category.

___ BALL

BASKET, BASE, FOOT, DODGE

A classic Connections-style compound-word category: add "BALL" to the end of each word and you get a sport. Slightly trickier than the first group since a couple of these words could plausibly hide in other categories before the pattern clicks.

British slang for money

QUID, DOSH, BREAD, DOUGH

QUID and DOSH are unmistakably British, but BREAD and DOUGH are common enough in American slang for money too — that overlap is exactly what makes this group harder to lock in with confidence.

Homophones of numbers

WON, TOO, ATE, FOR

The hardest group of the day. Read at face value, WON, TOO, ATE, and FOR look like they belong scattered across every other category — a verb, a preposition, a past-tense verb, another preposition — until you say them out loud and hear ONE, TWO, EIGHT, FOUR underneath.

Why these four groups

Today's puzzle leans on one of Connections' most reliable tricks: overlapping surface meanings. BREAD and DOUGH could sit in a baking-adjacent category; FOOT and BASE could sit in a measurement category; TOO, ATE, and FOR could all pass as ordinary function words at a glance. None of that is accidental — a good puzzle plants just enough plausible red herrings across groups that the easy category stays easy, and the hardest one stays genuinely hard until the wordplay clicks.
