How Scoring Works

The math behind the "How everyone did today" chart on every puzzle breakdown.

The basics

  • +1 to +4 Guessing a whole group correctly earns that group's own difficulty value — 1 for the easiest group, up to 4 for the hardest.
  • −1 Any other 4-tile guess — mixing tiles from different groups — costs 1 point, correct group or not.

You get 4 wrong guesses before the puzzle ends in a loss. A perfect, no-mistake win earns every difficulty's value at once: 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10. A total loss with no groups solved at all is −4.

Why some scores never show up

Once you've correctly solved 3 of the 4 groups, only one group's 4 tiles are left on the board — and since every guess has to be exactly 4 tiles, there's no other combination left to pick. Your next guess is guaranteed correct. That means it's mathematically impossible to still be sitting at "3 solved" when a 4th wrong guess would end the game — reaching 3 correct always becomes 4 correct (a win) first.

That one fact rules out three scores entirely — 4, 5, and 6 can never happen, which is why the bar chart on each post's breakdown skips straight from 3 to 7 with a dotted line marking the gap.

Nothing about you is stored

The only thing Foursure ever records is an anonymous count for each date-and-score pair — "3 people got a 7 on August 29th," for example. There's no account system, no identifying information sent or saved, and nothing about an individual game is ever retrievable after the fact.
